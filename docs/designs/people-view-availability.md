## Problem Statement

The People view is one of the most used parts of the planner and the natural home for
two jobs: **resource planning** (who is doing what) and **event planning** (when is a
suitable time to do something). It does the first job. It does not do the second — and
that job currently leaves the application entirely.

Separately, pressing **Today** does not centre the view on today. It guarantees only
that today is somewhere inside the window, which at some zoom levels puts it against an
edge.

## Demand Evidence

Internal tool, real users, so the evidence is behaviour rather than market signal.

- **The scheduling conversation happens in Slack, email or a meeting.** Asked what people
  do today to find a suitable time, the answer was that they leave the app, decide
  elsewhere, then come back and type the event in. The planner tells them who is busy; it
  does not tell them when everyone is free.
- **Today-does-not-centre is described as universally missed.** Not one person's
  preference — the common complaint.
- **Click-to-create is missing and was named unprompted** as a feature people expect in
  the person view.

Measured in the repo, for today 2026-08-31, showing where today actually lands in the
window `navigateToToday` produces:

| Zoom | Window | Today sits at |
|---|---|---|
| Year | 2026-01-01 → 2026-12-31 | 66% |
| Quarter | 2026-07-01 → 2026-10-01 | 66% |
| Month | 2026-08-01 → 2026-09-01 | **97%** (right edge) |
| Week | 2026-08-31 → 2026-09-07 | **0%** (left edge) |

Centred would be 50% in every row. The cause is `viewportForLevel(center, level)` in
`viewport.ts:32`: the parameter is named `center` and the function never centres — it
snaps to calendar boundaries (month start, Monday, Jan 1).

## Status Quo

To answer "when can we all do this", someone opens Slack, asks, waits, reconciles the
replies, then returns to the planner and enters the activity by hand. The planner is
used as a record of the decision, not as the tool that makes it.

The workaround costs a round of asynchronous messaging per event, and its answer is only
as good as whoever happened to reply.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**The user is a coworker who needs a specific set of people in the same window** — a
workshop, a review, a shared task — and currently has to ask them.

**The wedge:** pick the people who must attend, see the stretches where none of them has
anything scheduled, and create the activity in that stretch without leaving the view.

Explicitly excluded from this pass: capacity/load heatmaps, a constraint-query bar,
ranking of near-miss windows, and any integration with an external calendar.

## Constraints

- **Day granularity.** `Activity.startDate` / `endDate` are `YYYY-MM-DD` (`types.ts:80`).
  Availability is whole days. There is no time-of-day, so no meeting-hour arithmetic.
- **Every activity needs a lane.** `laneId` is required (`types.ts:77`). A People row is a
  person, not a lane, so creating from a person row cannot infer where the activity lives.
- **Recurrence must be expanded.** `people-renderer.ts:301` already calls
  `expandOccurrences`. Any availability calculation must use the same expansion or it will
  disagree with what the row draws.
- **Air-gapped deployment, no new runtime dependency.** Consistent with the rest of the
  project. Client-side only; ships through the existing tarball.

## Premises

Verified in the repo this session:

1. **The People view already has the input half.** `filterState.selectedPeopleIds`
   (`types.ts:116`) and the "Visible people" picker already let a user choose an exact set
   of people. The wedge needs no new selection UI.
2. **The People view already supports drag.** `setDragCommitHandler` (`people-renderer.ts:66`,
   wired at `planner.ts:530`) moves and resizes existing activities, with date↔x maths at
   `people-renderer.ts:214`. What is missing is only *creation*, not pointer interaction.
   An earlier reading of this session said the view had no drag; that was wrong.
3. **The three views share one window.** `timelineWidth` is fitted to the container
   (`people-renderer.ts:210`, `list-renderer.ts:89`) — the timelines do not scroll
   horizontally. The viewport window *is* the view, so a centring fix in `viewport.ts`
   fixes disc, list and people at once.
4. **The data supports a real answer.** Absence — leave, holidays — is recorded as
   activities in this planner, so the absence of an activity genuinely means available.
   This is what makes a free/busy calculation honest rather than a guess.

Decided this session:

5. **Busy = a non-cancelled, non-milestone activity the person is tagged on**, expanded
   across recurrences. Cancelled work is not happening; a milestone is a marker, not
   occupied time. Counting either would hide real windows and make the feature look broken.
6. **The lane is prefilled and editable, never silently chosen.** Default to the lane the
   selected people appear in most within the current window, falling back to the first
   visible lane. Shown in the dialog before saving.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Free band + click to create
Compute free windows inline in the People view, draw a summary row, make it clickable.
Effort S. **Not chosen:** the availability arithmetic ends up tangled in rendering, where
it cannot be tested and can be quietly wrong. That is the one part where being wrong
costs trust.

### Approach B: Availability as a tested module *(chosen)*
Extract the free/busy maths into a pure, DOM-free `availability.ts` with its own test
suite; the People view consumes it. Effort M.

### Approach C: Ask the question, not the grid
A constraint bar (duration, people, deadline) returning ranked candidate windows.
Effort M/L. **Not chosen:** the largest new surface and the most speculative — it assumes
people would rather query than look, which nothing observed supports yet. Its ranking of
near-miss windows ("Bo has one clash") is the genuinely good idea in it and is recorded
below as a later step.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B.** The deciding argument is empirical rather than architectural: twice in the
preceding session a pure module with a real test caught an error that reading the code did
not. `fitLabel` returned a 164-unit label for a 162-unit arc, and the contrast model was
wrong in *both* directions across two drafts. Free/busy is the same kind of arithmetic —
interval merging across recurrence expansion, off-by-one at window edges — and it is the
part a user cannot check by eye.

1. **`client/src/availability.ts`, pure and DOM-free.**
   - `busyIntervals(activities, personId, window): Interval[]` — expand recurrences via
     `expandOccurrences`, keep activities where the person is in `taggedUsers`, drop
     cancelled and milestones, clip to the window.
   - `mergeIntervals(intervals): Interval[]` — coalesce overlapping and adjacent days.
   - `freeWindows(perPersonBusy, window, minDays): Interval[]` — invert the union, keep
     runs of at least `minDays`.
   - Day granularity throughout, inclusive of both end dates. No `Date` mutation.

2. **Its test before its renderer wiring.** The matrix that matters: window boundaries
   (activity starting before / ending after the window), adjacent-day coalescing,
   single-day gaps, recurring activities crossing the edge, cancelled and milestone
   exclusion, a person with no activities at all, and an empty people set. The test must be
   shown to fail — a suite that cannot fail proves nothing.

3. **A FREE summary row in the People view**, drawn from `freeWindows` for the currently
   selected people, using the existing `dateToX` maths.

4. **Click or drag the free band to create.** Opens the existing activity dialog with the
   selected people tagged, the dates set from the band (or the dragged sub-range), and the
   lane prefilled per premise 6.

5. **Reconcile cancelled activities visually.** Premise 5 excludes cancelled work from
   busy, but the People view still *draws* it — nothing filters it today. Without a visual
   change a cancelled box would sit inside a green free band and read as a contradiction.
   Dim or hatch cancelled boxes so the band and the row agree.

6. **Centre Today — at Week, Month and Quarter only.** Replace the snap with a window
   centred on the date. **At Year zoom, keep the calendar year.** The `disc-as-clock`
   design makes the year ring meaningful: January at 12 o'clock, the today hand pointing
   at the real position. A rolling twelve-month window centred on today would put January
   at an arbitrary angle and break the thing the disc is for. Today at 66% of a calendar
   year is correct; today at 97% of a month is not.

7. **A minimum-duration control** on the free row ("≥ N days"), because a one-day gap
   between two projects is not a workshop slot.

## Open Questions

1. **Does "everyone free" mean everyone selected, or everyone required?** A workshop often
   has two essential people and four optional ones. Strict intersection may return nothing
   on a set of six. Ranking near-misses (Approach C's good idea) is the answer if so, but
   it should be driven by watching the strict version fail first.
2. **What happens when the answer is "no window exists"?** An empty band is the most
   likely outcome for a large set over a short horizon, and it is the moment the feature
   either helps or gets abandoned. It needs a designed empty state, not a blank row.
3. **Does the free row belong in the List view too?** The maths is view-agnostic by
   construction. Whether the List view wants it is a separate question.
4. **Discipline risk.** Premise 4 holds only while teams keep logging absence. One team
   quietly stopping degrades the feature silently, and nothing currently detects that.

## Success Criteria

- Free/busy is computed by a module with no DOM dependency, and its test suite is shown to
  fail when the logic is broken.
- The free band never contradicts what the rows draw — the same recurrence expansion feeds
  both, and cancelled activities are visually distinct from blocking ones.
- Selecting a set of people and reading off a candidate window takes one interaction and no
  Slack message.
- An activity can be created from a free window without retyping dates or people, and its
  lane is visible before saving.
- Pressing Today puts today at the centre of the window at Week, Month and Quarter zoom,
  and leaves the calendar year intact at Year zoom.
- No new runtime dependency; ships through the existing air-gap tarball.

## Dependencies

- None external. The availability calculation is arithmetic over data already loaded.
- Item 1 (the module and its test) lands before items 3–4, so the renderer consumes a
  tested function rather than growing its own copy.
- Item 5 (cancelled reconciliation) must land with item 3, not after — shipping the band
  without it ships a visible contradiction.
- Item 6 (Today centring) is independent of everything else and can ship on its own.

## The Assignment

**Ask three people who have scheduled something recently to describe the last time they
did it — and specifically whether the set of people was fixed or negotiable.**

Open Question 1 is the one that decides whether this feature works in practice. A strict
intersection across six people is likely to return nothing, and if the real behaviour is
"we moved it because Bo could not make it", then near-miss ranking is not a later step —
it is the feature, and the strict version will look broken on first contact.

It costs three conversations and it is the difference between building the wedge and
building the wedge that gets used.
