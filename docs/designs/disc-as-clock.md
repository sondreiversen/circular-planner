# Design: The Disc as a Clock

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-08-25
Branch: main
Repo: sondreiversen/circular-planner
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Builder

## Problem Statement

The circular planner is feature-complete and well built, but the disc only exists inside a logged-in browser tab. It is the project's whole differentiator — nobody else renders a year as a disc — and it has no life outside the app. The goal is to turn the disc into something the team actually sees, without turning this into a product effort. This is explicitly a side project; craft satisfaction is a legitimate success condition.

## What Makes This Cool

The disc is already a clock face and has been the whole time: a 12 o'clock seam, concentric rings, a today-indicator that is functionally an hour hand. It has never been allowed to tick.

The unlock is making `today` an input rather than a constant. Once time is something you pass in instead of something the renderer reads from the system, the same engine produces every output:

- **Locked to real time** → a live wall display
- **Swept fast** → a flyover of the whole year, exportable
- **Frozen** → a print-quality vector poster
- **Dragged** → a scrubber that pulls the year back and forth under the hand

Four capabilities, one abstraction.

**Honest caveat on "ticks."** At Year zoom the indicator advances about 0.0007° per minute — roughly 0.004 px of tip movement at `OUTER_RADIUS = 350`. Nobody will ever perceive it moving, any more than you watch an hour hand move. The value is that it is *always correct without anyone touching it*, and that day-to-day changes (activities crossing into the past) are visible across visits. Perceptible motion is what the flyover is for. This design does not claim otherwise.

## Constraints

- Side project, for fun. No revenue or adoption bar.
- Air-gapped, single-binary deployment. **No new npm runtime dependencies** — this rules out third-party video muxers and is the binding constraint on the flyover.
- Save model is debounced last-write-wins full sync. No realtime infrastructure, and none should be added.
- Audience is coworkers, so output has to work inside the org, not on the public internet.

## Premises

1. Making `today` injectable is worth doing first regardless of what gets built on top — it is the shared spine of every option considered.
2. This is a craft project. "My team found it delightful" is the bar; adoption metrics are not.
3. The disc rendering is done. The gap is delivery, not rendering.
4. **Contested — carried forward unresolved.** That "alive" is the axis that matters. See Open Questions.

Prediction: "the clock is a craft reward for the builder, and the team will find it charming for about four days."

**Where this design disagrees.** That critique is calibrated for a product. This is explicitly a fun project and a craft reward is the stated goal. ## Landscape Finding

Office information radiators fail on **relevance**, not staleness. People disengage within about a week when the screen shows information belonging to someone else's world. Successful ones are large, glanceable, and change periodically enough to be worth revisiting. If nobody acts on what it shows, it is wall decoration.

The uncomfortable implication, which this design does not resolve: a year-view disc is structurally the lowest-relevance daily glance you could build. Roughly 95% of what it shows is months away. The clock hand makes it *change*, which the research says helps — but change and relevance are different axes, and only relevance is the documented killer.

Sources: [Lunatech](https://blog.lunatech.com/posts/2005-10-07-information-radiators-dashboard-wall), [IxDF](https://ixdf.org/literature/topics/information-radiators), [Shift](https://www.shiftplatform.tv/post/how-to-display-kpi-dashboards-on-digital-signage-in-your-office)

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Clock spine, two thin outlets
`clock.ts`, then `?display=1`, then an SVG download button. Three small features sharing one time source. Effort S/M, risk low. Ships fast and each step is independently verifiable, but display and flyover become unrelated query-param branches with no scrubbing. **Retained as the fallback if the time budget collapses.**

### Approach B: First-class render modes
An explicit `RenderMode` in `renderer.ts`, each mode owning its theme and tick policy. Effort M/L, risk medium. **Ruled out:** touches the most load-bearing file in the project and most of its payoff stays invisible until a fourth mode exists.

### Approach C: One engine — time-scrubbing *(chosen)*
Everything becomes a pure function of `now()`. Display is the scrub locked to real time; flyover is the scrub swept fast; poster is the scrub frozen with vector output. Effort M, risk medium.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach C.** It is the only option where choosing both outlets costs less than choosing one. Under A the display and the flyover are two separate builds; under C they are one mechanism with a speed knob. It also makes date-dependent rendering deterministic, which the project has never had.

The risk is that the abstraction has to be right up front, and any stray `new Date()` outside `clock.ts` silently breaks determinism. Mitigation is step 2.

## Next Steps

1. **`client/src/clock.ts`** — export `now(): Date`, honouring an optional `?now=<ISO>` override read **directly from `window.location.search`**, not via `url-state.ts` (`decode()` is only imported by `planner.ts:24`; the public page uses raw `URLSearchParams` at `index-public.ts:23`).

   Replace these **seven** today-semantic call sites: `renderer.ts:918`, `planner.ts:331`, `planner.ts:1778`, `viewport.ts:272`, `list-renderer.ts:194`, `people-renderer.ts:342`, and `dashboard.ts:36` *(dashboard is included for consistency only — it renders no disc and is unreachable from any mode here; skip it if you prefer a tighter diff)*.

   **Deliberately leave `renderer.ts:356` alone.** It is the midnight-timer arithmetic, not the indicator. Swapping it means a swept `now()` in the past yields a negative delay, `setTimeout` fires immediately, `fullRender()` re-arms it, and you get a render loop. The midnight scheduler reads wall-clock; the indicator reads `now()`.

   Also leave: `toast.ts:121,138` (log timestamps), `pointer-drag.ts:56,57,60,61` (drag initialisers), `planner.ts:1216` (export filename). That is 7 occurrences across 3 files, plus the midnight timer — 15 `new Date()` calls total in `client/src/`, of which 7 get injected. (`admin.ts:178` uses `Date.now()` as a cache-buster; not a today source, leave it.)

   **`planner.ts:331` needs the same care as the midnight timer.** It is the paste-date anchor. Under a swept `now()` during a flyover, a paste would silently relocate activities. Either gate paste behind "clock is live" or have it read wall-clock directly.

2. **Guard the abstraction** — a CI grep rejecting `new Date()` in `client/src/` outside an **explicit call-site allowlist** (not a file allowlist: `planner.ts` contains both an injected site and a preserved one, so a file-level rule fails on day one). There is no ESLint in this repo, so grep is the only option. Depends on P1.

3. **`?display=1`** — in `index-public.ts`, read the param and set a `display-mode` body class. Hide `.app-header` (`app.css:156`), `.public-view-banner` (`app.css:998`), and `.cp-toolbar` — two files, not one.

   **Do not write the toolbar rule against `circular-planner.css:734`.** That line is inside the `@media print` block. The live `.cp-toolbar` rules are at 176, 426, 433, 671, and 711, several of which are responsive overrides that will beat a naively-placed hide rule on specificity. Write the `display-mode` rule to win against those, not against the print block.

   Let the SVG fill the viewport. Poll `/api/public/planners/{token}` every 60s, compare `config.updated_at` (per P2), and re-render via `Planner.setData()` (per P4) only on change.

4. **SVG download** — wire the existing `serializeSVGWithStyles()` to its own button in the `exportGroup` block (`planner.ts:1181-1228`). Place it **outside the `canvasSupported` guard** — `canvasSupported` is declared at 1208 and the `if` is at 1212; SVG export needs no canvas, so the new button belongs before that branch. Roughly ten lines. (There is an unrelated second `canvasSupported` at `planner.ts:491`; don't confuse them.)

   Caveat: no `@font-face` is embedded (system fonts only, per the file's own header), so text re-flows on a machine without the same fonts. Fine internally; do not treat it as a portable print master.

5. **Print theme — the existing print CSS is dead code for the disc.** `circular-planner.css:744-747` sets `--cp-disc-bg-inner/outer: #ffffff !important` under `@media print`. It has never worked, and not for the reason you would guess.

   `renderDefs()` reads those variables through `cssVar()` (`renderer.ts:268-271`) at **render** time and bakes the results into `stop-color` **attributes** on the gradient stops (`renderer.ts:284-285`). The disc is then filled with `url(#cp-bg-grad)` (`renderer.ts:366`). A CSS custom property override cannot retroactively rewrite an attribute that was already serialized. So printing from a dark-mode session yields a dark disc, and exporting yields one too — same root cause, both paths.

   (`serializeSVGWithStyles` reading screen-media `getComputedStyle` at `svg-export.ts:50,72` is real but secondary. Note also that `STYLE_WHITELIST` omits `stop-color`; the gradients survive only because they are attributes, which is exactly why the palette is stuck.)

   The fix is to re-render the defs against an explicit palette rather than ambient CSS — parameterise `renderDefs(palette)` and pass a forced-light palette for both capture and print. This is a genuine bug fix, not a feature, and it is worth doing whether or not the rest of this design proceeds.

6. **Flyover — re-scoped, and not free.** The original plan (`MediaRecorder`) **does not work**: there is no `SVGSVGElement.captureStream()`, and the only stream sources are canvas, `getUserMedia`, and `getDisplayMedia` (which needs a picker dialog and captures browser chrome). The existing rasteriser is also far too slow for real-time capture — `serializeSVGWithStyles` calls `getComputedStyle` on every descendant and round-trips through Blob → `Image` → async decode, hundreds of ms per frame. Three viable paths, none free:

   - **Animated SVG** *(recommended)* — `<animateTransform type="rotate">` on the hand plus CSS keyframes. Self-contained single file, no encoding, no canvas, no new dependency, air-gap clean. Plays in any browser. **Does not embed in PowerPoint.**

     This works because `renderTodayIndicator` (`renderer.ts:918`) is the only today-dependent element in the disc render, and the hand is a center-origin `<line>` — so a rotate transform reproduces the sweep exactly. **Hard precondition: the viewport must stay fixed for the whole sweep.** Year-zoom navigation slides month-by-month, and if the flyover moves the window then all grid and arc geometry changes, which SMIL cannot express. A fixed full-year viewport is the only configuration this path supports.
   - **`canvas.captureStream(0)` + `track.requestFrame()`** — works, but needs the rasteriser optimised first (hoist `getComputedStyle`, redraw only the hand and arcs) and frames are still wall-clock timestamped.
   - **WebCodecs `VideoEncoder`** — best output, requires a third-party WebM muxer, which **violates the no-new-dependency constraint**. Rejected.

   Note this weakens the "it gets pasted into the deck" rationale: PowerPoint does not play VP8/VP9 WebM, Safari/QuickTime playback is unreliable, `MediaRecorder` MP4 is Chrome-only, and GIF is out of scope. Choose the format against where it actually has to travel before building this.

7. **Scrubber — stretch, explicitly droppable.** A drag control that moves `now()`, reusing `pointer-drag.ts` patterns. It produces no artifact and travels nowhere, so it does not serve the stated problem; it exists because it is a good demo. Cut it first if time runs short.

8. **One determinism test.** The stated payoff of step 1 is deterministic date rendering; ship one test that proves it.

   **Scope it to pure functions.** Jest here is `testEnvironment: "node"` and `jest-environment-jsdom` is not in `devDependencies` — a test that renders and reads indicator angles needs a DOM that does not currently exist. Test `now()` honouring `?now=` and the angle-scale math directly, rather than adding jsdom. (Adding it is permitted by the constraint since it is dev-only, but it changes the air-gap npm install, so only do it deliberately.)

Explicitly out of scope: server-side rendering, chat integrations, proximity brightness curves, GIF encoding, device provisioning, and any settings UI. Display configuration is URL query params, which has the useful property that you can text a configuration to someone.

- `now()` is injectable and no un-allowlisted `new Date()` remains in `client/src/`.
- `?display=1` on the public page renders chrome-free and stays current for a week without anyone touching it.

## Open Questions

1. **Is "alive" the right axis?** Unresolved, deliberately. The landscape research and the second opinion both say relevance beats liveliness, and a year-view disc is inherently low-relevance for a daily glance. This design proceeds because the stated goal is craft, not adoption — but it is a real open question.
2. **Is there actually a screen?** The display assumes a mountable screen exists. If not, the file outlets carry the design and step 3 drops down the list.
3. **Where does the flyover have to play?** Determines the format in step 6, and the answer may be that no acceptable format exists without a new dependency.
4. **Does the flyover survive a sparse year?** If the plan is thin, a sweeping hand mostly crosses empty disc, and emptiness becomes the message.

## Bugs fixed (2026-08-25)

Three defects found during this session were fixed immediately rather than deferred:

1. **CI never ran the Go backend on `main`** (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`). Removed the `go-backend` branch gate on the `go` job. Replaced the stale `node` job — which spun up a Postgres service and ran the non-existent `npm run build` — with a `client` job running `npm ci`, `npm run build:client`, `npm test`. The Postgres service and `DATABASE_URL`/`JWT_SECRET` env were leftovers from the retired Node backend; the jest suite is pure client-side unit tests. **Client job verified locally: build succeeds, 24/24 tests pass. The Go job is unverified — no Go toolchain on this machine.**

2. **Optimistic concurrency control was silently disabled.** Worse than originally documented. Both `GET /api/planners/:id` (`handler.go:412`) and the public endpoint nest `updated_at` inside `config`, but `index.ts:53` destructured it at the top level, yielding `undefined`. That left `DataManager.lastKnownUpdatedAt` unset, so `client_updated_at` was never sent, so the server's 409 guard (`handler.go:506`, which only fires when the field is non-empty) was skipped entirely. **Two people editing one planner: the second save silently clobbered the first, with no conflict warning.** Fixed by reading `config.updated_at`, adding the field to `PlannerConfig`, and correcting the misleading `PlannerTimestamp` doc comment.

3. **The `@media print` disc palette was dead code.** `renderDefs()` bakes CSS custom properties into `stop-color` *attributes* at render time (18 `cssVar` reads in `renderer.ts`), so no CSS override can reach them. Dark sessions produced dark PNGs, dark SVG and dark printouts. Fixed with `Planner.withLightPalette()`, which flips `data-theme`, re-renders defs, runs the capture synchronously (no paint, so no flash), and restores — deliberately bypassing `applyTheme()` so nothing is persisted to localStorage. `exportSVGToPNG` was split into `serializeSVGWithStyles` + `rasterizeSVGString` so serialization happens inside that window. The print button got the same treatment via its existing `afterprint` cleanup.

   Verified in headless Chromium with a standalone harness reproducing the exact bake pattern: a print-media var override left the baked attribute unchanged, while a theme flip plus re-render produced the correct light values and restored cleanly.

**Not verified end to end.** There is no Go toolchain on this machine and assets are strictly embedded (`//go:embed public`), so the app could not be rebuilt and run. The changes typecheck (13 pre-existing `tsc` errors before and after — unchanged), build, and pass the test suite.

**Also noticed:** `npx tsc --noEmit` reports 13 pre-existing type errors and is not run in CI at all, because esbuild does not typecheck. Worth adding as a CI step.

## Spine landed (2026-08-25)

Steps 1, 2 and 8 are implemented. `now()` is injectable, the abstraction is
enforced in CI, and date-dependent rendering is deterministic for the first
time in this project's life.

**`client/src/clock.ts`** — resolution order is `setNow(d)` → `?now=<ISO>` →
wall clock. `parseNowOverride(search)` is split out as a pure function so the
URL rules are testable under `testEnvironment: "node"`, where `window` does not
exist. `now()` returns a fresh `Date` on every call and `setNow()` copies its
argument: `Date` is mutable, and sharing an instance would let one caller's
`setDate()` corrupt the clock for every other reader. `setNow`/`isLive` ship
now rather than later because they are what makes this a spine — display pins
the clock, flyover sweeps it, the poster freezes it.

**Six call sites migrated**, not the seven the plan listed: `renderer.ts:918`,
`planner.ts` (handleAddEvent seed), `viewport.ts:272`, `list-renderer.ts:194`,
`people-renderer.ts:342`, `dashboard.ts:36`.

**`planner.ts`'s paste anchor was carved out instead of migrated.** The plan
flagged it as needing a decision and left it open; the decision is wall clock.
Paste is a write, not a view, and its meaning must not change because someone
moved a slider. Recorded here so step 7 revisits it deliberately rather than by
accident — if the scrubber should make paste land on the scrubbed date, that is
a real design choice, not a missed migration.

**`scripts/check-clock-usage.sh`** fails CI on any `new Date()` in
`client/src/` that is neither inside `clock.ts` nor carrying a same-line
`// clock-exempt: <reason>` marker. Enforcement is by call site, as the review
required — a file-level allowlist would fail immediately, since `planner.ts`
holds both a migrated site and two exempt ones. The marker is deliberately
inline so the justification sits where the next reader will see it. Nine
exemptions exist today: the midnight scheduler, the paste anchor, the export
filename, two log timestamps, four drag placeholders, and one test assertion.

The guard was verified by making it fail: a probe `new Date()` added to
`viewport.ts` was caught and reported with file and line, and the check went
green again once removed. That run also caught a real omission — the new test
file's deliberate wall-clock comparison, which had gone unmarked.

**`client/src/__tests__/clock.test.ts`** — 17 tests. Beyond the parsing and
pinning rules, it proves the actual point: `navigateToToday()` reads `now()` and
is otherwise pure, so pinning the clock pins the render. Same pin twice gives an
identical viewport; 2026 and 1998 give different ones; Week zoom on a pinned
Saturday lands on that week's Monday. Suite is 41 tests across 3 files, all
passing. `tsc --noEmit` holds at its pre-existing 13 errors, none in the new
files.

**Still not verified end to end.** No Go toolchain on this machine and assets
are embedded via `//go:embed`, so the app has not been rebuilt and run. What is
verified: the guard fails and passes correctly, the suite passes, the client
bundle builds.

## Prerequisites closed (2026-08-26)

**P3 — `encode()` now carries params through, and this was a live bug.** The
plan framed it as a blocker for future mode params. It was already breaking
share links: `syncUrl()` runs on every zoom, navigation and filter change, and
`encode()` rebuilt the query string preserving only `id`. A public share-link
viewer who applied any filter lost `?token=` from their URL and hit "No public
link token provided" on their next refresh — with the reload, not the filter,
looking like the cause.

`PASSTHROUGH_PARAMS` now carries `id`, `token`, `display`, `now` and `flyover`.
State-owned params (`z`, `from`, `to`, `q`, `hl`, `lb`, `tu`, `sp`, `vm`, `cb`)
are deliberately excluded: they are re-derived every call, and passing them
through would let a cleared filter resurrect itself from the old URL. Eleven
tests cover it, and the regression tests were verified by reverting the fix —
three fail without it.

**P4 — `Planner` has a lifecycle, and the teardown surface was worse than
scoped.** The review found five leaking handles; there were more, and one leaks
during normal use rather than at teardown. `buildViewsControl()` registers a
document-level `click` listener, `buildToolbar()` calls it, and
`applyViewState()` calls `buildToolbar()` on **every saved-view load**. Loading
twenty saved views left twenty stale listeners on `document`, each holding a
dropdown that no longer existed. A half-finished `_globalKeyHandler` field was
already there, stored and never used by anything.

Document listeners are now tracked in two buckets by lifetime: `instance` ones
are removed by `destroy()`, `toolbar` ones are cleared at the top of every
`buildToolbar()`. `Renderer.destroy()` clears the midnight timer and its Escape
handler; `DataManager.destroy()` clears the debounce and subscribers, and
deliberately does **not** flush — a pending save belongs to an instance being
torn down, and firing it would race the replacement under last-write-wins.
`Planner.setData(data, updatedAt)` lets the display refresh on its 60s poll
without a teardown-and-rebuild cycle that would leak on every tick.

**P5 — resolved as: the display gets its own labelled token.** The plan costed
this as "migration plus handler plus share-UI change". It is cheaper than that:
the schema already supports many tokens per planner, since `token` is the
primary key, `planner_id` is only a foreign key, and `revoked_at` is per-row.
`ListShareTokens` already returned all of them. The single blocker was
`CreateShareToken` deliberately returning any existing active token instead of
minting a new one.

Migration `018-share-token-label.sql` adds a nullable `label` column — needed
not for multiplicity but for telling tokens apart, since the list previously
returned only opaque random strings. `CreateShareToken` is now idempotent **per
label**: an unlabelled request behaves exactly as before, so the existing public
link is untouched; a labelled request only ever matches its own label. The share
panel filters to the unlabelled token, without which creating a display token
would have swapped the URL shown there and made "revoke the public link" kill
the wall display instead.

**Verification status.** Frontend: guard passes, bundle builds, 52 tests across
4 suites pass, `tsc --noEmit` holds at its pre-existing 13 errors with none in
touched files. **Backend: unverified.** There is no Go toolchain on this
